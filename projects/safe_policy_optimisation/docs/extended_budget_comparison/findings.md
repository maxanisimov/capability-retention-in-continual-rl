# Extended-budget PSPO vs RL baselines — findings

Written 2026-08-26. Tables and figures in this directory are regenerated by
`scripts/compare_extended_budget_pspo_vs_baselines.py` and
`scripts/plot_extended_budget_comparison.py`; this file is the interpretation
and is not overwritten by either script.

## What the extended budgets change

The standard tables in `docs/pspo_reward_improvement_results.md` trained Bridge
Crossing v2 baselines for 200k steps and MiniPacman baselines for 500k, and
concluded that on v2 PSPO "remains below all reward-competitive baselines". That
conclusion does not survive the extended budgets. **The v2 gap was a budget
artefact, not a method limitation.**

| Bridge Crossing v2 | Reward (2SE) | Safety (2SE) | Seeds solved (reward >= 0.99) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| PSPO, 400k (standard) | 0.309 +/- 0.302 | 1.000 | 3/10 |
| PSPO, 1.6M (extended) | 0.900 +/- 0.200 | 1.000 | 9/10 |

PSPO triples its reward and reaches 9/10 solved seeds while keeping a perfect
unshielded safety rate. The single failure is seed 0, which is stuck at reward
0.0 in both budgets — it is a bad initialisation, not slow convergence.

## Bridge Crossing v2: where PSPO now stands

At the extended budget, ranked by reward:

| Method | Budget | Reward (2SE) | Safety (2SE) | Solved |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| PPO-Shield | 1.0M | 1.000 +/- 0.000 | 1.000 +/- 0.000 | 10/10 |
| PPO | 1.0M | 0.977 +/- 0.004 | 0.977 +/- 0.004 | 0/10 |
| PSPO | 1.6M | 0.900 +/- 0.200 | 1.000 +/- 0.000 | 9/10 |
| PPO-Lagrangian | 1.0M | 0.829 +/- 0.218 | 0.935 +/- 0.106 | 3/10 |
| PPO-PID-Lagrangian | 1.0M | 0.798 +/- 0.266 | 0.998 +/- 0.003 | 8/10 |
| CPO | 1.0M | 0.698 +/- 0.305 | 0.998 +/- 0.004 | 6/10 |
| PPO-Shield-Nominal | 1.0M | 0.399 +/- 0.207 | 1.000 +/- 0.000 | 0/10 |

Reading this table:

1. **PSPO beats every penalty-based safe-RL baseline** on reward
   (+0.07 over PPO-Lagrangian, +0.10 over PPO-PID-Lagrangian, +0.20 over CPO)
   and is the only one of the four at a 1.000 safety rate. The reward
   differences are inside 2SE — with a Bernoulli-like per-seed outcome and 10
   seeds the error bars are wide — but the solved-seed counts (9 vs 3, 8, 6)
   carry the same ordering and are not noise-limited in the same way.
2. **PPO's 0.977 is not a solve.** Every PPO seed lands at ~0.98, never 1.00,
   and its safety is 0.977: it takes a route that occasionally falls off the
   bridge. It buys ~0.08 reward over PSPO with 2.3% violating trajectories.
3. **PPO-Shield's 1.000 is the shield's, not the policy's.** PPO-Shield is the
   only row evaluated with its runtime shield attached. `PPO-Shield-Nominal` is
   the same trained policy evaluated unshielded, which is the like-for-like
   comparison against PSPO: it drops to **0.399** reward. PSPO's 0.900 at 1.000
   safety is a property of the exported policy, so PSPO is the better method
   whenever the shield cannot ride along at deployment.
4. **CPO is saturated.** Its extended-budget result is bit-identical to the
   standard one on all 10 seeds despite 5x the data (1683 vs 345 training
   episodes). More budget will not move it.

## MiniPacman

PSPO also improves substantially with the longer budget, and unlike v2 it is
already the strongest intrinsically-safe policy at both budgets:

| Method | Budget | Reward (2SE) | Safety (2SE) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| PSPO (extended) | 2.0M | 0.956 +/- 0.019 | 1.000 +/- 0.000 |
| PSPO (standard) | 500k | 0.839 +/- 0.069 | 1.000 +/- 0.000 |
| PPO-Shield | 500k | 0.947 +/- 0.051 | 1.000 +/- 0.000 |
| PPO | 500k | 0.924 +/- 0.032 | 0.217 +/- 0.069 |
| PPO-Shield-Nominal | 500k | 0.735 +/- 0.072 | 0.449 +/- 0.077 |
| PPO-Lagrangian | 500k | 0.592 +/- 0.074 | 0.972 +/- 0.017 |
| PPO-PID-Lagrangian | 500k | 0.584 +/- 0.090 | 0.975 +/- 0.022 |
| CPO | 500k | 0.005 +/- 0.007 | 0.998 +/- 0.004 |

At 2M steps PSPO reaches 0.956 at a perfect safety rate — above unshielded PPO's
reward (0.924) while PPO is unsafe on 78% of trajectories, and above PPO-Shield's
*shielded* 0.947. Stripping the shield (PPO-Shield-Nominal) leaves 0.735 reward
and 0.449 safety, so the intrinsic-safety argument is even stronger here than on
v2.

This also overturns the standard-budget MiniPacman conclusion that
"PPO-Shield has much higher reward at the same measured safety".

Note on which PSPO run is the control: the 500k row here is
`pspo_adaptive_mini_pacman_safe_entropy_w1_min0p95_freq100` (0.839), the matched
control for the 2M extended run. It is *not* the 0.554 figure in
`pspo_reward_improvement_results.md`, which comes from a different retained run
(`pspo_adaptive_two_hidden_directional_replace_all_margin2_200iters`). The
extended run reuses the safe-entropy base policy, so 0.839 is the correct
like-for-like starting point; against the 0.554 retained policy the extended
result is a larger improvement still.

**Caveat: the MiniPacman baseline comparison is not budget-matched.** The
extended-budget MiniPacman baseline sweep has no results — see below — so the
baseline rows above are the 500k standard-budget numbers. PPO-Lagrangian,
PPO-PID-Lagrangian and PPO-Shield may improve at 2M. Treat the MiniPacman
comparison as provisional until that sweep is rerun.

## Budget matching

Bridge Crossing v2 compares PSPO at 1.6M against baselines at 1.0M — PSPO gets
1.6x the environment steps. This does not change the two conclusions that matter:
PPO-Shield is already at a ceiling of 1.000 at 1.0M, and CPO is provably
saturated. It does weaken the PSPO-vs-Lagrangian reward comparison, which should
either be rerun with matched budgets or reported with the step counts stated.
